Here's what changed in Reliafy over the last few weeks. Everything below is
live now.

## Try it without a CSV

Fitting a model used to start with "upload a file". Now you can start four ways:
upload a CSV, **paste values** straight from a spreadsheet, **enter parameters**
you already know (from a handbook, a datasheet, or another tool), or pick one of
the **sample datasets** and see a full analysis in a click.

## Coming from Weibull++? One checkbox

Reliafy uses the survival-analysis convention for censoring: 0 means the unit
failed, 1 means it was still running. Many tools and spreadsheets use the
reverse. The column mapper now has a **"my censor column uses 1 = failed"**
checkbox, so your existing data fits correctly without editing the file.

## Share an RBD with anyone

Reliability block diagrams can now have a **public read-only link**, the same as
models and datasets. Whoever you send it to sees the diagram and its results —
reliability curve, MTTF, B-lives, importance, or availability for repairable
systems — without needing an account.

## Optimal overhaul interval

For repairable systems fitted with Crow-AMSAA, enter what a minimal repair costs
and what an overhaul costs, and Reliafy works out the **overhaul interval that
minimises your long-run cost**, how much it saves against never overhauling, and
the full cost curve. If the system isn't wearing out, it tells you there's no
optimum rather than inventing one.

## Which block is driving your downtime?

Repairable (availability) diagrams now show, for every block, its **share of
system downtime**, which blocks trigger system trips, and which ones hold up
restoration. Mean up time, mean down time and failure frequency are now exact
long-run values rather than simulation estimates, and the availability curve
now shows a confidence band.

## Two new worked examples

The RBD library has two new samples: an **instrument air system** modelled two
ways. The design case shows 2-out-of-3 compressors, a cold-standby dryer and a
common-cause failure group; the operations case adds repair times to every block
and reports availability. Open them, change a number, and watch the result move.

## Fixes

- Diagrams containing standby blocks can now be calculated. They're solved by
  simulation, and the validation panel says so.
- Duane growth models now report the correct growth verdict and failure rate.
- The assistant now models redundant equipment (A/B pairs, standby, N+1) in
  parallel rather than in series, and flags any failure data it had to guess.
