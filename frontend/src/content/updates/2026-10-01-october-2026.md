Here's what's new in Reliafy. Everything below is live now.

## Use Reliafy from your AI agent

Reliafy now works inside Claude and other AI agents that support MCP. In
**claude.ai** (or the Claude desktop and mobile apps), go to Settings →
Connectors → Add custom connector, enter `https://reliafy.com/mcp`, and sign in
to Reliafy. In [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code) it's one command:

```
claude mcp add --transport http reliafy https://reliafy.com/mcp
```

Then just ask: "what's the reliability of my pump model at 2,000 hours?",
"build an RBD of this conveyor with the A/B actuators redundant", "what's the
optimal replacement interval for this bearing?". Your agent works with your own
models, datasets and diagrams, using the same conventions and checks as the app,
and you can see and revoke its access any time under Settings → Connected apps.

It's **free to start**, with 50 tool calls a day. **Reliafy Agent**, a new
US$2/month plan just for AI agents, raises that to 2,000 a day with more room
for saved models and diagrams. **Pro** includes everything, including fitting
and simulation. Setup and the full list of tools are in the [API docs](/api-docs).

## Download any diagram as Python

Every reliability block diagram now has a **Download as Python** button. It
gives you a standalone script that rebuilds the diagram with the open-source
[SurPyval](https://github.com/derrynknife/SurPyval) and
[RePyability](https://github.com/derrynknife/RePyability) libraries — every
block's distribution, repair time, voting gate, standby and common-cause group
spelled out — and runs the same calculation. The numbers match what Reliafy
shows you.

Use it to check the maths, drop a diagram into your own pipeline, or run a
bigger analysis on your own machine. It works on your diagrams, shared ones,
the samples, and public links, on every plan.

## Availability: results you keep, and what's driving your downtime

Repairable (availability) diagrams now show, for every block, its **share of
system downtime**, which blocks trigger system trips, and which ones hold up
restoration. Running a simulation **saves the result with the diagram**, so
anyone you share it with sees it straight away, and Reliafy knows when a change
makes it out of date.

Availability simulation runs thousands of Monte-Carlo replications, so it's now
part of **Pro** (or available with purchased credits). Everyone can still build
and validate repairable diagrams, view saved results, and explore the worked
instrument-air example in full. And because the maths is open source, anyone can
download a diagram as Python and run the simulation themselves for free.

## Also new and improved

- **Share an RBD with anyone:** reliability block diagrams can have a public
  read-only link, like models and datasets. No account needed to view it.
- **Optimal overhaul interval:** for repairable systems fitted with
  Crow-AMSAA, find the overhaul interval that minimises long-run cost.
- **Start without a CSV:** paste values from a spreadsheet, enter parameters
  you already know, or open a sample dataset.
- **Coming from Weibull++?** The column mapper has a "my censor column uses
  1 = failed" checkbox.
- **Fleet alerts (Pro):** get an email when a fleet's expected failures cross a
  threshold or jump, with usage rates estimated from the readings you send the API.
- **Two new worked examples:** an instrument air system as a design case
  (2-out-of-3 compressors, cold-standby dryer, common cause) and with repair
  times for availability.
- **Exact long-run values:** mean up time, mean down time and failure frequency
  for repairable diagrams are now exact, and the availability curve shows a
  confidence band.
- **Faster:** pages load only what they need, and the app starts up quicker.
- Diagrams containing standby blocks can now be calculated.
- Reliability charts size their time axis to the system rather than its
  longest-lived part.
- Duane growth models report the correct growth verdict and failure rate.
- The assistant models redundant equipment (A/B pairs, standby, N+1) in
  parallel rather than in series, and flags any failure data it had to guess.
