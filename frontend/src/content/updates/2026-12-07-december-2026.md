---
title: "What's new in Reliafy — December 2026"
date: 2026-12-07
summary: Safety functions now show each element's true share of the PFDavg, with common cause on its own row. Confidence bands on block diagrams now carry every fitted block's uncertainty. And standby blocks with constant failure rates are now exact.
---

Some numbers in Reliafy have changed this month because they are now more
correct. Here's what moved and why. If none of these are things you use,
your results are unchanged.

## Numbers that are now more correct

- **Where the PFDavg comes from (safety functions):** the **Share of
  PFDavg** column now adds up to 100%. Before, each channel of a redundant
  pair was given the whole of the time both channels were down together,
  and a common cause was counted inside each member's share, so the column
  added up to more than 100%. Now the two channels split the time they are
  down together, and each common-cause group has its own share on its own
  row: what the PFDavg would fall by if that shared cause never struck. For
  example, two transmitters voting 1oo2 with a 5% common cause, a logic
  solver and a trip valve: each transmitter was shown at about 18% and is
  now under 1%, and the common cause is 16% on its own row. The single
  elements in series (the valve and the logic solver) barely move. The
  PFDavg itself is unchanged.
- **Confidence bands on block diagrams** now carry the uncertainty of every
  fitted block. A block using a regression model (a model with covariates),
  a degradation model, a mixture model, a Royston–Parmar model or a
  repairable model used to be held at its fitted values when the band was
  worked out, so the band was too narrow and claimed more certainty than the
  data gave. These bands are now wider, and correct. The diagram's own
  figures are unchanged.
- **Confidence bands for blocks fitted with an offset** (a 3-parameter
  fit, "nothing fails before a set time, then a Weibull from there"). To
  work out the band, Reliafy redraws each block's model many times. Each
  redrawn offset fit carries its own offset, but the band kept the original
  offset and paired it with the redrawn shape and scale, so the band was
  mostly far too wide. For example, a 3-parameter Weibull fitted to 40
  lives: the 90% band on its reliability at 900 hours was 0.47 to 1.00 and
  is now 0.76 to 0.91. The diagram's own figures are unchanged; the band
  and the ranges on its mean life and B-lives move.
- **"Chance a spare starts" on standby blocks** now means what it says: a
  spare that fails to start is skipped and the next spare is tried. Before,
  a failed start ended the whole standby group, as if the switch itself had
  failed. That only matters with two or more spares, so results rise for
  those blocks. For example, one pump running and two cold spares, each
  starting 9 times in 10: the reliability at the units' mean life was 0.848
  and is now 0.881. Warm and hot standby now take the chance a spare starts
  too (before, they ignored it and assumed every spare started), so results
  fall for those blocks when it's below 100%: two warm spares at 9 in 10
  went from 0.864 to 0.827. Warm standby with two or more units running,
  and cold standby with spares of their own model and three or more
  running, are now worked out exactly when the units have constant failure
  rates (Reliafy used to refuse them).
- **A Bayesian update used as a block** in a diagram now follows the same
  curve as its own model page. The block used to be built from the
  middle values of the update's parameters, which gives a tidier, more
  hopeful curve than the update really supports when it rests on only one
  or two failures. On a typical update with two failures, the chance of
  failing by 500 hours went from 2.3% to 3.0% and the B1 life from 313 to
  235 hours. These blocks now carry a band too: a credible band, from the
  update's own range of plausible parameters, and the diagram says so.
  The diagram's **Download as Python** and its RePyability JSON download
  now match the screen for these blocks too: they refit the update from
  the failures and the starting model saved with it, instead of writing
  in the middle values, so the script prints the same reliability the
  diagram shows.
- **As of now with common cause:** when a block in a common-cause group
  has a current state (its age, or down since a time), the simulation now
  keeps the group's shared failures in. Before, it left the group out and
  said so beside the results.
- **Standby blocks** whose units have constant failure rates (exponential
  lives) are now worked out exactly. Results move by about 0.00001 at most
  (0.0001 for two different units operating).
- **Bootstrap bounds** (the bootstrap option on accelerated life models,
  and Buckley–James regression) are now read at exactly the confidence
  level you ask for. They are a little wider than before and now hold
  that level.
- **Fine-Gray competing-risks models** now report robust standard errors,
  as R's `crr` does. The coefficients are unchanged; their standard errors
  and intervals can move.
- **Zero-inflated fits** now count units recorded as failed somewhere
  between 0 and a later time, or data truncated at 0, as possibly dead on
  arrival. The share dead on arrival can come out larger.
- **Repairable-system models** and some other fits now give the same
  answer whatever time unit the data is in. The changes are in the sixth or
  seventh significant figure.
- **The model Calculator's answer at a time** is now the model's own value
  at exactly the time you type, with its bound worked out at that time too.
  Before, both were read off the chart's curve by drawing straight lines
  between its plotted points, which is close where the curve is gentle but
  wrong where it bends sharply, often early in life. For example, a
  lognormal fitted to ten lives (seven failed, three still running) said
  95.6% reliability at 100 hours, and we're 90% sure at least 89.8%; it now
  says 96.3%, and at least 86.0%. The old bound claimed more certainty than
  the data gives. The same goes for the table when you compare covariate
  values, and for the answer having survived to an age. Times at a
  reliability (B-lives) were already exact and are unchanged.
- **One failure count per dataset:** a dataset that keeps no reading of its
  own is now counted on the Data list and its page the way its newest model
  read it, so the list, the page and the fit agree. Before, a status column
  named "failed" holding only 0s showed "0 failed" on the list while the fit
  counted every row as a failure (0 means failed in Reliafy). When a
  column's name and the way it's read disagree like that, the fit now says
  so, with "Read it as 1 = failed" one click away.
- **Repairable or not, read from the rows:** a repair history where one
  failure was logged twice at the same hours is now read as repeat failures
  of each unit (Repairable), not one life per row. For example, 14 trucks
  with 150 failures were shown as "150 lives · 150 failed" and now read as
  "14 trucks · 150 failures". Start and stop rows for one life (each row
  starting where the unit's last one stopped, with the load over time)
  are now read as one life per unit with time-varying covariates, not as
  repeat failures, so 80 motors split into 286 rows count as 80 lives.

## Also new and improved

- **Two measures of use:** a part that wears by two clocks at once (hours and
  starts for a motor, flight hours and landings, hours and cycles) can now be
  fitted from each unit's two totals, in your own units. Reliafy works out
  what one start, landing or cycle is worth in hours and gives the life at any
  rate, with bounds. Use it in block diagrams at a set rate (the diagram's 90%
  band includes the fit's uncertainty, the worth of a start too), and in
  Strategy and the RCM calculators ([how](/guides/two-clocks)). Free. New, so
  no existing numbers change.
- **Overhaul and replace:** a new Strategy page for a repairable unit finds
  how often to overhaul it and after how many overhauls to replace it, for
  the lowest long-run cost, with running to failure, overhauling only and
  replacing only beside it. Units follow the model (flight hours stay flight
  hours); exact, so free. It's also under a repairable model's Plan ahead, and
  in the Explorer a model's ⋯ menu offers **Add a strategy…** with the tools
  that fit it. New, so no existing numbers change.
- **Libraries:** making and sharing libraries is part of Pro; public
  libraries are free to use. On any plan you can browse public libraries and
  copy their datasets and models into your workspace.
- **Repairable models as blocks:** a model fitted on Models › Repairable
  (Crow-AMSAA, Duane, Cox-Lewis, ARA, ARI, G1) can now be a block in a
  repairable diagram, with a rule for when the unit is replaced with a new
  one: at an age, or after a number of repairs. Reliafy suggests the best
  rule for the block's costs, and says when a model never wears out and so
  needs no rule. New, so no existing numbers change.
- **Standby as plants run it:** a standby block now takes two or more units
  running with warm spares ("2 running, 1 warm spare ageing at 30% while
  waiting"), and units that differ: an older duty pump, a smaller spare, each
  on its own model. Constant-rate units are worked out exactly whatever the
  arrangement; where no exact answer exists (warm Weibull spares with two
  running, say) the block says so plainly and can be simulated with Pro. New,
  so no existing numbers change.
- **Loads that change over time in repairable diagrams:** a block on a
  covariate model can now follow a schedule in a repairable diagram too. The
  schedule belongs to the plant: a repaired or replaced unit carries on under
  the load in force at that time. These figures are simulated (Pro).
- **Covariates that change within a mission phase:** a phase's covariate can
  follow a schedule over the phase (a day-and-night duty cycle, say) instead
  of one value, in the mission tree and the RBD builder alike. Worked out
  exactly, the same as splitting the phase into shorter ones.
- **Schedules that repeat on more than one cycle** (a daily and a 30-day
  pattern together) are now recognised as repeating, so they are followed to
  any time, and their mean life (MTTF) is now given where it was missing.
- **Data list counts match the fit:** the count beside each dataset ("10
  units · 0 failed") now reads the status column the way you set it up for
  fitting (which value means failed, or 1 = failed), and inspection data
  (failures found between two inspections) is tagged non-repairable. Before,
  units still running could be counted as failed. Intentional change: some
  counts and tags on the Data list are different, and now correct.
- **Phased missions from now:** when you set the blocks' ages with As of now,
  the phased mission now starts from those ages too. Before, it stayed at the
  from-new figure. Intentional change: a mission flown by worn blocks now
  shows a lower chance of completing, which is correct.
- **Missions with true standby:** a block on standby in a mission phase is
  now idle until the phase needs it (when the block it backs up fails), then
  runs, may fail to start (set its chance of starting), and, if warm, wears
  while it waits. Before, its clock simply stopped and it could never fail,
  which was optimistic. Missions are also drawn as one curve through every
  phase, with the blocks that drive each phase's failures. Intentional
  change: missions with a block on standby come out lower, and correct. For
  example, two duty and standby pumps and a valve through a 10-hour start-up
  and a 500-hour duty: 89.9% before, 81.6% now. Missions without standby are
  unchanged. Constant-rate blocks are worked out exactly; other standby
  missions are simulated with Pro.
- **Placeholders counted all the way down:** an RBD built from what's inside
  a position now counts the placeholder blocks inside its sub-systems too
  ("27 of 27 basic blocks are placeholders", not "1 placeholder"), and says
  so in red when most of the answer rests on them. A mission no longer
  treats a sub-system made only of placeholders as a block with a model: it
  isn't named the weakest block, and there's no "from the blocks with models
  alone" figure unless one really exists. Intentional change: those figures
  now disappear or name fewer blocks, which is correct.
- **One rate per part:** a position with no life model but parts on its parts
  list that a saved parts count rates now takes that rate. In an RBD built
  from the tree it's a constant-rate block marked "from the parts count"
  instead of a 1,000-hour placeholder; in the FME(C)A, a failure mode with no
  rate typed uses it for its criticality, and a typed rate is shown as an
  override beside the parts count's. Intentional change: such blocks and
  criticalities now use the parts count's rate (a motor at 30 failures per
  million hours instead of a 1,000-hour placeholder).
- **Parts counts say what they cover:** a parts count that leaves lines out
  (parts that aren't electronic, or have no handbook type) says so beside its
  MTBF ("electronics only: 3 lines left out"), and its figures are rounded
  once, the same everywhere. A built item's copy of its product's parts count
  is shown as a parts count, with its breakdown.
- **Block importance names every block:** a block called "engine" lost its
  importance figures and showed only its id. It now shows its name and
  figures like every other block.
- **Fewer false unit warnings:** a block fitted in flight hours (or any
  running hours) on an availability diagram in hours no longer warns that
  its units aren't converted when you've set how much of the time it runs.
  Its life is counted in running time, as intended.
- **Maintenance and proof tests in running hours:** in an availability
  diagram's Maintenance plan, any task, proof tests included, can now be
  counted in running hours ("test every 2,000 running hours") as well as on
  the calendar, with the calendar time it comes to shown through the block's
  Runs share. Choosing the intervals answers in the task's own unit, and a
  task due by use on an hours counter in the asset tree comes through in
  running hours. Replacements already given in running hours give the same
  numbers as before.
