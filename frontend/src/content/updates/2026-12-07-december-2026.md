title: "What's new in Reliafy — December 2026"
date: 2026-12-07
summary: Safety functions now show each element's true share of the PFDavg, with common cause on its own row. Confidence bands on block diagrams now carry every fitted block's uncertainty. And standby blocks with constant failure rates are now exact.
---

A few numbers in Reliafy have changed this month because they are now more
correct. If you use safety functions, standby blocks or confidence bands on
the models below, here's what moved and why.

## Numbers that are now more correct

- **Where the PFDavg comes from (safety functions):** the **Share of
  PFDavg** column now adds up to 100%. Before, each channel of a redundant
  pair was given the whole of the time both channels were down together,
  and a common cause was counted inside each member's share, so the column
  added up to more than 100%. Now the two channels split the time they are
  down together, and each common-cause group has its own share on its own
  row: what the PFDavg would fall by if that shared cause never struck. For
  example, two transmitters voting 1oo2 with a 5% common cause, a logic
  solver and a trip valve: each transmitter was shown at about 18% and is
  now under 1%, and the common cause is 16% on its own row. The single
  elements in series (the valve and the logic solver) barely move. The
  PFDavg itself is unchanged.
- **Confidence bands on block diagrams** now carry the uncertainty of every
  fitted block. A block using a regression model (a model with covariates),
  a degradation model, a mixture model, a Royston–Parmar model or a
  repairable model used to be held at its fitted values when the band was
  worked out, so the band was too narrow and claimed more certainty than the
  data gave. These bands are now wider, and correct. The diagram's own
  figures are unchanged.
- **As of now with common cause:** when a block in a common-cause group
  has a current state (its age, or down since a time), the simulation now
  keeps the group's shared failures in. Before, it left the group out and
  said so beside the results.
- **Standby blocks** whose units have constant failure rates (exponential
  lives) are now worked out exactly. Results move by about 0.00001 at most
  (0.0001 for two different units operating).
