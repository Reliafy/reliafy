This month's three headlines all start from the data you already have:
condition readings, your shutdown calendar, and the test you're about to
plan.

## Condition monitoring: each unit's remaining life, updated as readings come in

Most reliability models give every unit in a population the same risk at
the same age. But you know more than its age. You have vibration readings,
wear measurements, oil analysis, temperatures. **Remaining life from condition readings** puts
the readings and the failure history together, so each unit gets its own
risk of failure, and that risk updates every time a new reading arrives.

Give it two tables: one row per unit (its age, whether it failed, and
anything else you know about it, such as its load or duty), and the
readings taken over each unit's life. Reliafy fits how the readings change
over time and how they drive failure. You get:

- **each running unit's risk** of failing in the next 30, 90 and 365 days,
  and its median remaining life;
- a **unit view**: its readings so far, where they're heading, and its
  updating survival curve;
- **what drives failure**, in plain sentences, for example how much each
  extra unit of vibration raises the risk of failure.

Then use a unit's prediction as a **block in a reliability block diagram**:
"how likely is this line to get through the next month, given what the
sensors say about each pump right now?"

Find it under Models → Condition monitoring. It's part of Reliafy Pro.

## Plan replacement around your shutdowns

The best replacement age is only useful if you can replace things at that
age. In practice, most planned replacements happen at a shutdown. The new
**Shutdown calendar** mode in Optimal replacement plans around the
shutdowns you already have, each with its own cost: weekly stops, a
six-monthly shut, the annual major.

- **The rule in plain words:** for example, with a shut every 180 days,
  "at the shut, replace the part if it's older than 138 days". In that
  example the plan costs about 20% less than running to failure, and 11%
  less than replacing at a fixed age.
- **The cost a year** compared with running to failure, plain age
  replacement, and replacing at every shut.
- **The next 12 months as a schedule**, with real dates.
- **One unit right now:** enter its age and see whether to replace it now,
  at the next shut, or leave it, with the break-even ages.

Exact answers are free on every plan.

## A test planner, before you spend the test budget

The demonstration test calculator is now a full **Test planning** page under
Strategy:

- **Demonstration:** how many units, for how long, to show a reliability or
  an MTBF at a given confidence, now also for failure-terminated tests.
- **Sequential:** accept or reject as the results come in, often with less
  testing than a fixed-length test.
- **Life test:** how precisely a test will estimate a B-life or the mean, or
  how many units and how long you need for the precision you want.
- **Accelerated test:** the best stress levels and how many units to put at
  each, or a check of a plan you have in mind. It opens pre-filled from any
  accelerated life model.
- **Comparison:** will a test be able to tell whether a change worked? Units
  and power for comparing two groups, and the operating time you need before
  and after.

All the plans are free. Checking a plan by simulation is part of Pro.

## The Reliafy API and command line now do everything your agent can

Everything your AI agent can do with Reliafy, a script can now do through
the [Reliafy API](/api-docs): fit, analyse, build and change diagrams,
plan tests, forecast fleets. There's also a command-line tool:

```
pip install reliafy-client
reliafy login
reliafy fit failures.csv --dist weibull
reliafy tools
```

It's handy for scheduled jobs, such as pushing a fleet's usage every night,
or for running an analysis over a whole folder of files. The API is part of
Reliafy Pro.

## Also new and improved

- **A new menu:** your work is grouped under **Design** (Systems: your block
  diagrams) and **Analyse** (Data, Models, Strategy and Forecasts), with
  **Reports** collecting your reports, saved analyses and shared links. Tell
  Reliafy what you do in Settings and the menu and Home put what you use
  first. Old links and bookmarks still work.
- **Life data:** B-lives and MTTF with confidence bounds; requirement
  checks; competing risks, now with a 95% band for each failure mode;
  time-varying covariates; more regression models; recurrent-event models.
- **Block diagrams:**
  - rename blocks and add them in place;
  - enter a block by its MTBF;
  - a starter for safety functions;
  - Kijima repair and mixture lives;
  - allocate a target across blocks;
  - production availability;
  - phased missions and networks;
  - time-varying conditions;
  - "What to improve" measures;
  - discounted costs;
  - exact Compare;
  - run history with time estimates.
- **Common-cause groups** with proof tests are now included in the exact
  failure frequency and up and down times.
- **Proportional-hazards check:** now the exact test used by R's
  `cox.zph`.
- **Reliability growth** projections with BC modes use the bias-corrected
  shape, which matches the handbook's worked example.
- **Uncertainty** for limited-failure and zero-inflated fits now includes
  the share that fails.
- **Additive-hazards models** now show a blank outside the model's valid
  range, rather than a reliability above 1.
- **Compare groups** now treats a blank failure mode as a unit still
  running, the same as fitting does. If you compared groups on data with a
  failure-mode column, it's worth running the comparison again.
